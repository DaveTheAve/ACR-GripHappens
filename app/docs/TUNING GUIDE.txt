TUNING WITHOUT GUESSWORK

Recommended is the tested public gameplay setup, not a guaranteed real-world component lifespan.
Custom numbers are pending until Apply. Section Reset restores only that section to
Recommended. Original-game choices and Recovery are different actions.

RateScale: Controls tread consumption. Higher values use tyres faster; zero restores the
original disabled consumption setting for this supported build. Native worn-tyre grip is
retained. No fixed tyre lifetime is promised.

WetTarmac: Reduces the wet-tarmac loss coefficient. More reduction makes this part of
wet-road grip loss gentler, not that percentage more grip. Dry-road grip and tyre slip
curves are unchanged.

VisualDamage: Scales body-deformation input. A higher value can make dents more visible;
it does not multiply crash forces or mechanical health loss. Stock preserves ordinary
body damage.

CollisionDamage: Scales health loss from eligible contacts. Higher values are more
punishing; they do not increase impact forces. Recommended uses a separate 1.50x
override, shown beneath the preset.

TyreDamage: Enables native compression-related tyre damage. This is a processing switch,
not a puncture chance or a guarantee that every hit punctures a tyre. Other native
conditions remain in control.

MechanicalFailures: Determines how early a damaged component becomes eligible for the
native failure checks. Higher health thresholds permit checks after less damage. An 85%
threshold is NOT an 85% failure chance.

BrakeWear: Enables the native disc and pad consumption routine. Native temperatures,
braking forces and material limits determine the amount. No duplicate brake-fade penalty
is added.

Detachment: Scales break thresholds for authored breakable attachments. Smaller factors
allow earlier separation; unbreakable parts remain unbreakable. This is not restricted
to cosmetic panels.

CriticalDamage: Allows eligible collision and mechanical failures to reach the native
Critical state. It does not add fixed health loss or a random failure chance. Severe
damage can end a run.

CoolingRouting: Adds approximate engine-bay impact coverage when a cooling socket is
missing. Dedicated sockets take precedence. Optional modeled heat is a different control
under Engine and turbo response; it is not a native coolant-leak simulation.

EngineImpact: Weights collision damage reaching the Engine part. Higher values make
engine hits more consequential; they do not directly multiply horsepower loss. It
interacts with collision scaling and native health limits.

PowerResponse: Makes existing engine/turbo damage affect performance before complete
failure. Caps never increase the original response. Tune numbers contains separate
optional cooling and routine engine-wear controls; each is described independently.

WheelImpact: Distributes impact effects by distance to eligible wheel hubs. Bending and
optional toe/camber changes use actual wheel damage, without inventing controller
steering. Unequal damage can cause pull; symmetric damage need not pull to one side.

GearboxDamage: Enables grinding, engine-hit transfer and progressively reluctant
shifting. Recommended preserves healthy shifts and the softened internal-life response.
Severe component damage can still prevent shifts. Stress measures actual coupled
over-rev exposure, not a fixed penalty per paddle press.

Tyre consumption coefficient [1e-05 to 0.1 x]: Higher means faster tread consumption.
Overrides the selected coefficient, not a fixed lifetime or an extra grip penalty.

Wet-tarmac grip-loss coefficient [0 to 1.5 x]: Smaller means less wet-tarmac loss from
this coefficient. 1.00 retains stock; this is not a measured grip multiplier.

Deformation input [0.25 to 3 x]: Higher amplifies deformation input. Native limits
remain; it does not directly scale mechanical damage.

Collision health loss [0.1 to 5 x]: Higher scales eligible collision health loss.
Recommended is 1.50x. Contact geometry and clamping still matter.

Engine collision weighting [0.1 to 8 x]: Higher increases Engine-only collision
weighting. Recommended keeps 4.00x. Does not alter routine wear.

Engine response health divisor [0.1 to 1 fraction]: Remaining-health fraction below
which the extra engine cap acts. Higher begins performance loss earlier; it does not
change health.

Turbo response health divisor [0.1 to 1 fraction]: Remaining-health fraction below which
the extra turbo cap acts. Higher starts the cap earlier; not measured boost.

Engine-hit transfer to gearbox [0 to 1 fraction]: Share of actual engine collision
health-fraction loss transferred to the gearbox. 0.50 means half. Wear and repairs do
not transfer.

Native grinding damage rate [0 to 5 x]: Higher increases native grinding-wear input.
Clean shifts are not charged a fixed penalty.

Maximum added engagement time [0 to 3 seconds]: Higher adds delay to damaged ordinary
shifts. Healthy shifts retain native timing.

Minimum failed-shift recovery [0 to 5 seconds]: Minimum pause after a rejected damaged
shift, in seconds. Higher makes rapid retries less effective; longer native waits
remain.

Coupled over-rev exposure [0 to 1 s/rev]: Higher increases gearbox stress from actual
coupled downshift over-rev. Recommended keeps 0.12. No penalty merely for requesting a
downshift.

Damage-only steering deformation [0 to 6 x]: Higher amplifies only native damage-related
steering-joint displacement. It does not move the steering input.

Boosted deformation limit [0 to 0.05 native units]: Upper size of the added bending
amplification, in native length units. Existing larger native damage is not reduced.

Native gearbox wear affects shifting [0 to 1 enabled]: Lets internal gearbox life
contribute to shift difficulty using the softened response. Healthy timing stays normal;
component damage remains separate.

Differential zero-rate fallback [0 to 0.0001 native rate]: Fallback only when the
authored differential wear rate is zero. Higher increases eligible material use.
Existing nonzero rates and material limits remain.

Damage-sensitive added toe [0 to 3 degrees]: Maximum added toe angle, scaled by each
wheel damage level. Higher can increase damaged-car scrub or instability; it is not a
fixed steering pull.

Cooling damage and modeled overheating [0 to 1 enabled]: Enables the added
damage-sensitive cooling/heat model. Temperature is modeled, not native coolant
telemetry. No fluid-leak simulation is claimed.

Cooling efficiency health divisor [0.1 to 1 fraction]: Health fraction used by the
cooling-efficiency cap. Higher can reduce cooling efficiency earlier after damage; it
does not change impact health loss.

Modeled overheat wear exposure [0 to 5 x]: Higher scales added engine-wear exposure when
the model overheats. Zero removes this added heat penalty, not native over-rev or
collision damage.

Keep dashboard damage warnings in sync [0 to 1 enabled]: Makes dashboard warnings
reflect operational engine/gearbox degradation. Critical can mean currently unusable,
not necessarily permanently destroyed. No extra damage is added.

Damage-sensitive added camber [0 to 5 degrees]: Maximum added camber angle, scaled per
wheel and mirrored by side. Higher strengthens geometry consequences without
steering-input injection.

Routine engine-use wear [0 to 2 x native use wear]: Multiplies only routine Engine-use
wear. Recommended is 0.50x. Zero does not prevent collision, over-rev, boost, thermal
damage or other failure paths.
